Nearly two centuries of resilience, faith, and enterprise

Between 1834 and 1917, nearly 12 lakh Indians crossed the Kala Pani under girmit, the agreement many of them signed with a thumbprint. They served their five years in the cane fields, the mills and the lines, and kept their faith through it, from Ram Leela at Navua in 1902 to Hosay in Trinidad. When their indentures ended, most stayed. They sold produce at the bazaar, built homes on land of their own, raised temples, mosques and schools, and their descendants went on to lead their countries.
