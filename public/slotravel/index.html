How I travel

  1. 3 weeks, 4 to 7 bases. A plain apartment for anything over 3 nights.
  2. Sightsee fast, then stay put. (I once did all of Baku in half a day.)
  3. 1st class on trains, always. Flights unbundled - pay for the seat, skip the rest.
  4. No shopping stops. Food stops count as culture.
  5. Every science museum on the way. (The long-term goal is all of them.)
  6. A ride or a swim every few days.
  7. My passport stays with me. Never as a deposit.

Eurorail

Gate A

Girona → Paris → Munich → Vienna → Zurich → St. Moritz → Milan

Europe on 1 rail pass, start to finish. Girona is my base in Europe, so the trip starts there, crosses the Alps on 2 UNESCO railway lines and ends in Milan.

(Eurail retired its name on 10 Sep 2026 and the pass is called Interrail now. I am still calling it Eurorail.)

Day by day

  1. Days 1–4

    Girona (4 nights)

    Land in Barcelona, 38 min to Girona on the AVE. Ride Rocacorba in the morning, the old town and the Call (the Jewish quarter) after. 1 day back in Barcelona for CosmoCaixa (it has a flooded Amazon forest inside).

  2. Days 5–8

    Paris (4 nights)

    TGV direct from Girona, about 6 h. Musée des Arts et Métiers (Foucault's pendulum, and early airplanes hanging inside a church). Cité des sciences. A 2-hour Louvre speed run.

  3. Days 9–12

    Munich (4 nights)

    TGV direct from Paris, about 5.5 h. A full day at the Deutsches Museum, then its aviation branch at Flugwerft Schleissheim. Beer garden in the Englischer Garten.

  4. Day 13

    Salzburg (stopover)

    Bags in a station locker, 5 hours in town, back on the next Railjet to Vienna. Same pass day.

  5. Days 13–15

    Vienna (3 nights)

    Technisches Museum Wien. Coffee house afternoons. 1 day on the Semmering railway (opened 1854, often called the first true mountain railway), then walk the railway trail to the 20-Schilling view.

  6. Day 16

    Zurich (1 night)

    About 8 h on the Railjet over the Arlberg. Swim in the Limmat if it is summer.

  7. Days 17–18

    St. Moritz (2 nights)

    Up the Albula line from Chur, over the Landwasser Viaduct. Ride or hike in the Engadin.

  8. Days 19–21

    Milan (2 nights)

    The Bernina line down to Tirano on the regional train, windows open, past the Brusio spiral viaduct. Then on to Milan for the Leonardo da Vinci science museum. Fly home on day 21.

Good to know

  1. Pass: Interrail Global Pass, 1st class, 7 travel days in 1 month, about CAD 800. The Salzburg stop and Vienna share a travel day, and Barcelona is a separate cheap ticket.
  2. Book the 2 TGV seats as soon as booking opens. Pass-holder seats on French trains run out.
  3. Bernina line: the regional train needs no reservation and the windows open. The Bernina Express has bigger glass and a compulsory paid seat reservation.
  4. Best months: June or September.

Ecuador + Peru

Gate B

Quito → Quilotoa → Baños → Lima → Cusco → Sacred Valley → Machu Picchu

Volcanoes first, then the Incas. Ecuador is a slow ramp up in altitude before Cusco, and Lima sits in the middle for 3 days of eating at sea level.

Day by day

  1. Days 1–3

    Quito (3 nights)

    The old town (on the UNESCO list since 1978). The TelefériQo up to about 3,900 m to start the altitude ramp. The Museo Interactivo de Ciencia, in an old textile mill. Mitad del Mundo for the equator photo (the painted line is about 240 m off the real equator).

  2. Days 4–5

    Cotopaxi and Quilotoa (2 nights)

    Hacienda stay under Cotopaxi. Walk the Quilotoa crater rim, about 3,900 m up.

  3. Days 6–8

    Baños (3 nights)

    Rent a bike and roll down the Ruta de las Cascadas to the Pailón del Diablo waterfall (about 18 km, mostly downhill), then a truck back up. Hot springs after.

  4. Days 9–11

    Lima (3 nights)

    Fly Quito → Lima. Ceviche at lunch (cevicherías close in the afternoon). 1 big tasting menu (Central or Maido, booked well ahead). The Malecón bike path in Miraflores, then Barranco at sunset.

  5. Days 12–14

    Cusco (3 nights)

    Fly up to 3,400 m and go slow for 2 days. Qorikancha, Sacsayhuamán, San Blas.

  6. Days 15–16

    Sacred Valley (2 nights)

    Bike from Moray down to the Maras salt pans. Ollantaytambo.

  7. Day 17

    Aguas Calientes (1 night)

    Vistadome train from Ollantaytambo (rule 3). Early night.

  8. Days 18–21

    Cusco (3 nights)

    Machu Picchu at first entry, train back. Then Rainbow Mountain, about 5,000 m, as the last big day. Fly home on day 21.

Good to know

  1. Machu Picchu tickets are sold by circuit and entry hour on tuboleto.cultura.pe, tied to your passport. Book as soon as dates are set (they sell out months ahead in high season).
  2. Quito (2,850 m) and Quilotoa come first, so Cusco (3,400 m) is not a shock. Lima in the middle is a sea-level rest.
  3. Best months: May to September (dry season in the Andes).

Cultural Mexico

Gate C

Mexico City → Puebla → Oaxaca → Mérida

4 bases from Mexico City to Mérida, with 5 pyramid sites on the way. Oaxaca is the food stop (7 moles to get through) and Yucatán is the swimming stop (cenotes).

Day by day

  1. Days 1–6

    Mexico City (6 nights)

    Museo Nacional de Antropología (a full morning, minimum). Teotihuacán, at the gate when it opens. The murals at Bellas Artes. Universum at UNAM and MIDE (a museum of economics, very PM). Casa Azul, booked ahead. Sunday: ride Reforma with no cars (Muévete en Bici) on an Ecobici.

  2. Days 7–8

    Puebla (2 nights)

    ADO bus, about 2 h. The Great Pyramid of Cholula (biggest pyramid on Earth by volume, with a church on top). Mole poblano in its home town.

  3. Days 9–13

    Oaxaca (5 nights)

    ADO Platino, about 4.5 h (rule 3 covers buses too). Monte Albán. A swim in the mineral pools at Hierve el Agua. A mezcal palenque in Santiago Matatlán. Work through the 7 moles.

  4. Days 14–21

    Mérida (7 nights)

    Fly Oaxaca → Mérida (usually 1 stop). Uxmal and the Ruta Puuc. The Tren Maya to Chichén Itzá. Swim in the cenotes (Ik Kil, Cuzamá). Gran Museo del Mundo Maya. Sunday Biciruta on Paseo de Montejo. Fly home on day 21.

Good to know

  1. Best timing: the end of October, so Oaxaca lands on Día de Muertos (1 and 2 Nov). Book Oaxaca early for those dates.
  2. The Museo Nacional de Antropología and Casa Azul are closed on Mondays.
  3. The Tren Maya station at Chichén Itzá is outside the site, so plan the last stretch by bus or taxi.
