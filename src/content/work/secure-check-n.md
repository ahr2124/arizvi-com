- The constraint was the interesting part. Purpose-built screening machines were expensive and slow to ship in 2020, and every facility already owned a tablet. So the kiosk is a sensor and a frame, and the tablet is the computer.
- The app does the three things a receptionist actually needs. It shows pass or fail as a colour. It lets a manager change the alert temperature for their facility (37 °C by default). And it waits a set time between scans, so a queue at the door doesn't get double-counted.
- Check-in works by touch or by voice ("Zoey, check me in"), because nobody at a door in a pandemic wants to touch a shared screen.
- Senior living chains were the adopters that mattered. Their residents are the people most at risk, the front desk is staffed by non-clinical people, and screening has to run all day without slowing visitors down.
- Visitor tracking was optional and opt-in, with reports emailed monthly to a registered address, and nothing collected unless a facility turned it on.
- It sits in the same line as the rest of my work: technology built for the room it will actually be used in, rather than the lab it was designed in.
