When you ask Alexa something, a layer underneath decides which skill or domain gets to answer. I owned that layer. Every other Alexa team depended on it, and so did roughly ten thousand external skill developers.

The work was mostly about making that layer do things it was never designed for. Skill links let a customer talk to two skills at once, which unlocked routines, monetisation, and multi-skill experiences. Orchestration passthrough let other voice agents (Cortana, Portal) and celebrity voices run inside Alexa. Latency work gave every other service more headroom.

I built the annual and quarterly roadmap, held twelve SVP-level goals, and spent most days negotiating between business development, UX, and engineering teams across Amazon and a few partners outside it. It is the job that made me want to study how people actually use these systems, which is why the PhD came next.
