# arizvi.com

Astro site, hosted on GitHub Pages from `ahr2124/arizvi-com`. Every push to `main` builds and deploys via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`. `public/CNAME` holds the custom domain so it survives redeploys.

## Run locally
    npm install
    npm run dev        # dev server
    npm run build      # static build into dist/

- `/ablookup/` reads Canadian aviation weather abbreviations in plain English or French (`public/ablookup/index.html`, one file, sends `noindex`, not linked from the nav). Type one abbreviation, or paste a whole line, and it answers in plain text with the manual's own rows under it. The list is the "Alphabetical order of abbreviations" section of MANAB (Manual of Word Abbreviations, 5th edition, September 2026, Environment and Climate Change Canada), all 1,592 rows as the manual has them, typos included. It sits in the `<script id="manab">` block, one `abbreviation|English|French` row per line; for a new edition, replace those rows. The reading choices that are not the manual's (which meaning comes first, the plain connecting words, + and - as heavy and light) are the short lists at the top of the page's script. It also reads a plural S on entries the manual writes with "(s)" (TOPS, LYRS, KTS), and splits TAF wind and cloud groups (28012G22KT, BKN060) into words the manual has. The table under the answer shows one row for each meaning that is showing. A second section of the script, "Sentence rules", turns the word-for-word reading into a sentence: in French, describing words move after the noun and agree with it (ISOL CB reads "cumulonimbus isolés") and the small words go in (avec des, dans les, au-dessus de l'Alberta); in both languages, countable weather is plural, a bare number after TOPS, CIGS, FZLVL, ABV or BLW is hundreds of feet, a number with SM is a visibility and a number with Z is a time. Each rule works from short word lists (`ADJ`, `NOUN`, `PROV` and the like), fires only on words in them, and leaves the rest word for word. To teach it a word, add it to the list. `?q=` opens it on a line and `?lang=fr` in French. Its highlighter colour is aqua (#7DE3F4). A copy is on Netlify at ablookup.netlify.app (project `ablookup`): the page with the 4 font files, the favicon and the mark it uses, plus a `_redirects` line that sends /ablookup to /. It was dropped as a zip on the project's Deploys page, so it does not change when this file does; drop a new zip to update it.
- `/anh/` is Anh's GMAT tracker (`public/anh/index.html`, one file, sends `noindex`, not linked from the nav), in her colours: white, blue and pink, with deep blue in place of black. She does questions from a book or website and answers them here. A practice set (any number of questions, at the GMAT pace, her own limit or none) or a timed section (21 Quant, 23 Verbal or 20 Data Insights questions in 45 minutes; with test rules on she cannot go back, and at the end she reviews and changes up to 3 answers, one change per question visit) runs a clock that only counts while the set runs and charges the time to the open question, with her pace against the real one. She can bookmark, mark a guess, add a note, and paste the question to tap any word in it; words are looked up in her browser (English from dictionaryapi.dev, Vietnamese from MyMemory) and saved with the sentence they came from. After a set she pastes the answer key or marks each one, gives a reason for each mistake, and gets a chart of time per question. The trackers: Topics (every question type, and the maths, Critical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension topics, with how many she has done, how many are right, her time against the pace, and a status she sets), Exams (practice exam scores against her target), Mistakes (to redo until right), Bookmarks, Words (with flashcards) and a Log. Home ranks what to do more of: types and topics below her own accuracy for that section, slower than the pace, or not tried, plus her lowest section on the latest practice exam. Leaving the practice screen pauses the clock; if the page closed for over 10 minutes the clock stops where it last ticked and she can choose to count the gap. Everything is kept in the browser (IndexedDB) and synced to the storage API in `anh-api/` (below), so nothing is lost offline. The passcode is the `ANH_PASSCODE` environment variable on the Netlify project; change it there and redeploy to lock everyone out.
- `anh-api/` is the storage behind `/anh/`: one Netlify Function (`anh-api/netlify/functions/api.mts`) on Netlify Blobs, in the Netlify project `delicate-douhua-bf9bf7` (Bodhika team), which builds from this repo with base directory `anh-api` and only when something in that folder changes. It takes the passcode as a bearer token and answers only arizvi.com in the browser (CORS; more origins go in `ANH_ORIGINS`). Routes: `GET /api/ping`, `GET /api/list` (every key with its etag), `POST /api/get` (entries for up to 200 keys) and `POST /api/put` (writes and deletes in one call). Keys: `s/` practice sets, `e/` practice exams, `w/` words, `t/` topic notes and status, `m/settings`. The page's `API` constant points at the project.
- `/girmitiya/` is the picture archive for The Girmitiya Story, the exhibition at Pravasi Bharatiya Divas 2027 (`public/girmitiya/index.html`, one file, sends `noindex`, not linked from the nav). It holds every picture the Missions in Fiji, Trinidad and Tobago, and Guyana supplied, as a wall of pictures with tags to filter by country, subject (ships, plantations, women, faith and festivals, and so on), kind (photograph, postcard, document, exhibition panel) and period, plus search. Each picture opens in a viewer with its catalogue number (F, T or G by country, then section and place), caption, credit and tags. Every page of a PDF is a picture of its own (T11.6 p19, with the other pages of its document beside it in the viewer), titled from the page where it can be: each elder in Celebrating the Elders, each of the General Registers. Addresses can carry the filters and the picture, e.g. `/girmitiya/?theme=women&country=fiji#F7.15`, or `#T11.6/19` for a page. The page takes the exhibition's own identity rather than the site's: night blue (#0D1238) and marigold (#F2A93B), with Tiro Devanagari Hindi for display and Libre Franklin for text, both self-hosted in `public/girmitiya/fonts/` (SIL OFL). Its images are in `public/media/girmitiya/` (`full/` at up to 2000px and `thumb/` at 640px, WebP) and the hero is `public/girmitiya/hero.webp`. The page is generated from caption and tag data, so changes go through the build rather than by hand. Its copy is in Canadian spelling, to match the Missions' captions.
- `/girmitiya/twin/` is the digital twin of the approved exhibition design: a tour of the pavilion from its floor plan, gallery by gallery, with the renders, the Landing reliefs that light up with their projection, the films (with captions) and all thirteen drawing sheets. The design belongs to Belita and is confidential, so the whole page is password protected and sends `noindex`; the archive's footer links to it. `public/girmitiya/twin/index.html` is a password form holding the page encrypted (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations, then AES-256-GCM, decrypted in the browser like `/atmri/`), and every picture and film is a separate encrypted file in `public/girmitiya/twin/a/` (12-byte IV, then ciphertext and tag) that the page fetches and decrypts as it is needed. File names there are keyed hashes, so they say nothing about the contents. The plain page, the source files, the password and the build scripts stay outside this repo; to change anything, rebuild and re-encrypt rather than editing these files.

## Content (`src/content/`)
Fields for each collection are in `src/content.config.ts`.
- `work/*.md`: one file per write-up. `featured: true` puts it in the home grid; the markdown body is the write-up at `/projects/<slug>/` (there is no Work page since 11 Nov 2026; `/work/` addresses redirect); `order` sorts, and Next on a write-up follows it. A body that opens with a list gets a "Notes" label above it, so on a phone it does not read as part of the Path's last step.
- `projects/*.md`: one card each on /projects. `link:` points a card at its write-up. `image:` is the cover (3:2), `video:` an optional cover video for the case-study page only. Every entry needs a body: the logbook that listed the others was removed on 10 Nov 2026.
- Research, Publications, Teaching and About are written directly in their page files.

- Keep videos under about 25 MB (GitHub refuses files over 100 MB). A good default:
      ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf scale=1280:-2 -c:v libx264 -crf 30 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -c:a aac -b:a 96k out.mp4
